Job 1 of 4

Digsilent Buyisa

Junior Software Developer · Pretoria

Feb 2020 – Jul 2020 6 months Found on Indeed

How I got it

Straight off Indeed. I saw “junior developer”, applied, and didn’t even check what the company actually did.

What it was actually like

Not great, honestly. I thought I’d learn how development works in the real world – shipping features to production. It wasn’t like that. They were an electrical company that plans and monitors substations, and I was coding alerts in Java. Pretty boring.

Job 2 of 4

TypeDev

Junior / Intermediate Software Developer · Pretoria

Jul 2020 – Dec 2022 2 yrs 6 months Recruitment agency

How I got it

I applied while I was still at the first job (lol). I used a recruitment agency to help me find something new, and that’s how I landed the interview with TypeDev.

What it was actually like

Amazing. A complete 180. Much more aligned with what I wanted to do. Pure software development for clients in the finance industry. I learned to build backends, mobile applications and websites, and deploy them on AWS. I would not have left this company if the chance to work abroad hadn’t come up.

Job 3 of 4

CIMSOLUTIONS

Software Engineer · Netherlands

Dec 2022 – Sep 2023 10 months LinkedIn recruiter

How I got it

I always knew I wanted to work in another country, so I started casually applying everywhere until a recruiter from the Netherlands contacted me. They liked my profile and said I should apply for this job. I got the interview and made it in.

What it was actually like

I’m grateful they brought me over and sponsored my visa. But they were a consultancy, where you have to earn your clients and if you didn’t have one, you upskilled a lot. That did get me some certifications in the meantime, but coming from where I came from, I wasn’t happy sitting idle for so long.

Job 4 of 4 · where I am now

Nedap current

Software Engineer · Netherlands (Remote)

Sep 2023 – now 3 years LinkedIn recruiter

How I got it

Another recruiter contacted me on LinkedIn and offered me an opportunity with Nedap. I applied, they invited me for an interview, and this is where I am now.

What it was actually like

A big challenge. The company is very, very big. Over 3000 employees across many different departments. But honestly, the best company I’ve ever worked for to this day. I was in the Netherlands for three years, and now I work remotely with a couple of visits back to Europe every year.

Bridging the gap · Upskilling

Tools & methodologies

Version control

GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket.

They all basically work the same. Master one, and you can master them all.

Project management tools

Jira, Confluence, GitLab.

Where the requirements and information live: tickets, boards, who’s doing what, and relevant documentation (this is more important than you would think).

Agile / Scrum

  • Stand-ups
  • Sprint planning
  • Sprint refinements
  • Sprint reviews (demos)
  • Sprint retrospectives

Cross-team communication

You will not build anything alone. Cross-functional collaboration is always part of the job.

Bridging the gap

Certifications

DevOps

These helped me. Pick one: Azure or AWS.

Want to go further? There are more options such as Nomad and Kubernetes.

PSM

Professional Scrum Master. Proves you understand how a project should be managed and executed.

Language certs

Something like C# or Java.

Bridging the gap

Do your research

  • Know what you’re getting yourself into. Research the companies and know what they actually do.
  • Keep up with new technology. Subscribe to short newsletters. TLDR is good.
  • Connect with industry leaders on LinkedIn. Your feed becomes part of your learning.
  • Use AI :)

Tips

Ask the right questions

  • Which tools, technologies and frameworks do you use daily?→ work
  • What would my day-to-day tasks look like?→ work
  • How big is the team, and where would I fit into it?→ collaboration
  • How does the team make key project decisions?→ collaboration
  • Is this a remote, hybrid or on-site role?→ culture
  • How does the leave policy and time-logging system work?→ culture

An interview goes both ways. Asking these makes you look like someone who has done this before.

Tips

Four things I’d tell you

  1. Work with recruiters

    It's not just about getting the job, it’s making the right match between the company and you.

  2. Networking is NB

    The people who know your name are the ones who forward your name.

  3. Learn to understand

    Not just to pass. Once you’re in the workplace you don’t want to be the dev who doesn’t know what they’re doing. Nobody is going to hold your hand forever.

  4. Be proactive

    Go after what you want. Take the first steps yourself. The good companies love that.
